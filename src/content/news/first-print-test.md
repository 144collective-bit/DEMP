---
title: "Notes from the first print test"
date: 2026-08-11
summary: "Things that looked fine at 2000 pixels and did not look fine at 55 millimetres. A list, written down so we stop repeating it."
---

Screen art lies to you. It lies in a very specific direction: everything looks
sharper, brighter and more legible than it is going to be once it's a piece of
vinyl the size of a matchbox.

Here is what came back from the first proof, in the order it annoyed us.

## Thin strokes are not real

Anything under about two units of stroke weight simply stopped existing. Not
"looked thin" — stopped existing. The cutter doesn't care about your subtle
linework and neither does the ink.

Everything got fattened up. Nobody will notice on screen. Everybody would have
noticed on the sticker.

## Black on black needs a keyline

Obvious in hindsight. The whole range is drawn on black, so when it's cut out
and stuck on a black laptop lid the silhouette is gone and you've got a
floating face.

Every design in the range now carries a white die-cut edge. It also happens to
look better, which is the kind of accident we'll take.

## Small type is decoration

The word marks that read cleanly at full size turned into texture at sticker
size. Either the word is the whole sticker or the word isn't on the sticker.
There's no middle setting.

## The colours moved

Not badly, and not in a way that's worth chasing to the last percent. The acid
green went slightly duller and the cyan went slightly warmer. We adjusted the
two worst offenders and left the rest alone, because chasing an exact screen
match on a physical print is a way to spend a month achieving nothing.

## What we changed

Four designs redrawn, keylines added across the range, one design pulled
entirely because it only worked at poster size. It'll come back as a poster.

Next proof when it's ready. There will be a third one after that, because there
always is.
