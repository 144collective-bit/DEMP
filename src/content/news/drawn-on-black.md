---
title: "Why everything is drawn on black"
date: 2026-07-27
summary: "A short, boring, entirely practical explanation for the one design decision people keep asking about."
---

The answer nobody wants: because stickers get slapped on dark things.

Laptop lids. Road cases. The back of a monitor. Lamp posts at two in the
morning. Every surface this art was ever going to end up on is either dark or
about to be, and art drawn for white paper looks like a mistake the second it
leaves the screen.

So the whole range is drawn on black, cut on black, and the site is black
because the site is a catalogue and a catalogue should look like the thing it's
selling.

## The knock-on effects

Once you commit to that, a few things follow whether you like them or not.

- Every mark needs a keyline, or it vanishes into the background.
- The type has to carry weight, because thin strokes disappear at sticker size.
- There's exactly one theme. A light mode would mean redrawing every asset, and
  we're not doing that so somebody can look at us in a coffee shop.

None of this is a philosophy. It's just what happens when you draw for vinyl
instead of for a screenshot.
